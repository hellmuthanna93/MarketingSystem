# Anonymized case studies - Anna Hellmuth

Client stories build trust, but in a counseling context confidentiality is non-negotiable. Default to **pattern, not person**. Read `practice-voice` and `dach-mental-health-compliance` first.

## Core rule

Tell the **pattern**, never the person. Compose stories from recurring themes observed across many clients - never a single identifiable individual.

## What is allowed

- Composite or generalized patterns ("Many high-functioning clients arrive saying…")
- Published testimonials that are **anonymous and opt-in** (attribute as *- Former client*, with privacy note)
- Themes mirrored from `brand/marketing/messaging.md` testimonial themes (pattern awareness, crisis relief, warmth & safety, etc.)

## What is forbidden

- Names, ages, nationalities, professions, or locations that could identify someone
- Direct quotes from a private session without explicit written consent
- Disease-cure claims attached to a story (HWG - see compliance skill)
- Any detail combination that makes a real client recognizable to themselves or others

## Structure for a pattern story

1. **The recognizable arrival** - how this kind of client first describes the problem (composite)
2. **The pattern underneath** - what's really happening (the insight)
3. **What shifts with support** - the movement, honestly (no miracle, no guarantee)
4. **Soft invitation** - optional discovery-call CTA

## Testimonial handling

- Always anonymous: *- Former client*
- Include privacy note: *feedback from clients is posted anonymously to protect their privacy*
- Opt-in only; easy to decline (see `client-emails` testimonial ask)
- Keep to **experience and process**, not cure claims

## Voice

Warm, precise, grounded. The reader should think *"that's me"* - through the universality of the pattern, never through exposure of a real person.
