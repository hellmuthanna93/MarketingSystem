# Brand voice: Anna Hellmuth

**Practice:** Psychological counseling & life coaching  
**Site:** [annahellmuth.com](https://annahellmuth.com/)

## Tone

- Warm and understanding, **and** unmistakably confident that situations and inner states **can change**
- **Felt inner strength and compassion.** Steady, grounded presence; the reader senses both your firmness and your care.
- **Not separate from their struggle.** The reader should feel you have been there too; you know their experience from the inside out, not from a distance.
- Specialist voice: you understand your clients' problems deeply, know them inside out, and know exactly how to help them move through
- The reader should feel *this is written exactly about me*. Name their inner conflict, patterns, and stuck points with precision.
- The reader should feel you **know what you're doing.** They can trust you, and together you **will** get where they need to go.
- Grounded certainty, not hype: change takes work, but paralysis is not permanent and the path forward is knowable
- Speaks to **sensitive, thoughtful people** who understand deeply and genuinely strive for change, but cannot get there through insight alone
- Honest about limits (e.g. cannot guarantee specific outcomes) while fully confident in process, expertise, and what becomes possible with the right support
- **Clients are grown-ups.** Respected as intelligent adults who can hold complexity and make their own decisions.

## Tone (no-goes)

Never sound like:

- **Stupid motivational slogans** ("you've got this!", "good vibes only", "manifest your dream life"). Empty, hollow, beneath the reader.
- **Downplaying client experiences** ("it's not that bad", "everyone feels this way", "just think positive"). Never minimize what they carry.
- **Pitying the client / collapsing into their helplessness.** Do not over-sympathize, treat them as a victim, or sink into the stuckness with them; you feel real empathy *and* stay grounded as the one who can help them move.
- **Making yourself small.** No apologetic hedging, qualifying disclaimers, or shrinking from your expertise.
- **Overly protective parent voice.** Never talk down, coddle, infantilize, or treat clients like children who need rescuing.
- **Coldness or showing-off intellect.** No clinical distance, no academic posturing, no jargon as performance, no "look how smart I am".
- **Trying to impress.** No name-dropping methods, credentials, or theory to look credible; trust is earned through precision and presence, not display.

## Audience & ideal clients

Sensitive, deep-feeling adults who understand a lot about themselves and their situation, and still feel stuck. They strive for real change; self-awareness and reading about growth have not been enough to break through. International clients; sessions in **English, German, Ukrainian, Russian**, or a mix.

### Ideal coaching client

**Both men and women**, typically **30-45**, a skilled professional or thoughtful seeker who is curious and self-aware. **Goal-oriented doers**, used to acting and producing results, but not very aware of their own emotions. Often the **oldest child** in the family, with the over-responsibility and self-reliance that comes with it. High-functioning on the outside but craving something deeper. Often living abroad or in transition; seeking meaning, freedom, and authenticity beyond surface success.

They may have hit milestones yet feel something is *off*. Longing and fear pull in opposite directions. In the push toward goals they tend to **lose contact with themselves and their inner values**, over-functioning, rationalizing, pushing through. Wanting more but without the inner resources for new decisions, forcing themselves until burnout leaves them even more exhausted and disappointed.

**Some arrive already exhausted**, tired of old decisions and the life those decisions built, ready for change but **without the energy** to make it happen on their own. They know what no longer fits; they cannot find the fuel to step out of it.

**Common struggles:** paralysis, procrastination, emotional avoidance, fear of failure or success, difficulty deciding, identity confusion, blocked creativity, loneliness, lack of aligned community.

### Ideal psychological counseling client

Typically a **woman 25-45**; also **gay men**. A highly trained, sensitive professional in a demanding field. Many grew up with cold, emotionally unavailable, or absent parents and carry a deep sense of emptiness inside; they learned to suppress emotions and neglect their own needs.

Many arrive in **mild to moderate depression** with **anxiety and chronic overthinking**. They are **highly demanding of themselves** and carry a deep, often hidden, sense of **shame**. **They have lost their goals and orientation.** The old ways of coping, achieving, and holding it together no longer work, and they don't yet know how to live differently. They understand their history and patterns intellectually, but insight alone has not healed what they carry.

**Ukrainian women living abroad are one meaningful part of this client group**, building a new life, nostalgic for home, struggling to find deep connection in their adopted country. Other clients come from a range of backgrounds, including immigration histories from many nations and locals dealing with the same inner patterns.

**Relationships are one of the biggest pressure points.** Some are in serious romantic relationships; others are single and unable to build one. When the relationship is struggling, fear of losing the partner and being alone takes over, driven by an **insecure attachment style** rather than the reality of the bond. **Body image is another core wound.** Many do not like their own body and carry shame around it, even while investing carefully in their appearance.

**Common struggles:** mild-to-moderate depression, anxiety, overthinking, perfectionism and harsh self-demand, shame, lost goals and orientation (old ways no longer work), attachment-driven relationship fear, body image issues, exhaustion, using busyness or scrolling to avoid deeper feelings or difficult decisions.

## Writing guidelines

- Use **you** and direct address; avoid jargon unless explaining a method (CBT, hypnotherapy, etc.)
- Lead with recognition (*you know this feeling*) then confidence (*and it can shift*). Mirror the client's experience before offering the way through.
- Honor their intelligence and self-awareness. The gap is not lack of understanding, but that insight alone cannot create the change they want.
- Prefer full sentences and flowing paragraphs on web; bullets OK for steps and benefits
- Headings: sentence case or title case consistent with site (Source Serif 4, confident, not shouty)
- Avoid guilt-based pressure; emphasize choice and possibility ("Why not give it a try and find out?")

## Humanized writing (no AI tells)

Everything written for Anna (web, social, blog, email) must read like a thoughtful person wrote it, not a model. The brand already rejects hype and slogans; these rules push that down to the sentence level. Based on Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing."

### Hard rules

- **Never use em dashes or en dashes (`—` `–`).** This is the single most reliable AI tell. Replace each one with a period (start a new sentence), a comma (a tight aside), a colon (before an explanation), or parentheses, or just restructure. Also avoid spaced hyphens used as a dash (` - `, ` -- `); write a real clause instead. Scan every draft for `—` and `–` before it ships.
- **No emojis** unless explicitly requested.
- Straight quotes/apostrophes in markdown and code. The website's curly quotes are a fine typographic choice (curly quotes on their own are not an AI tell).

### Cut these patterns

- **Significance inflation:** "stands as a testament to," "marks a pivotal moment," "plays a crucial role," "reflects a broader." Say the plain thing.
- **Promotional filler:** vibrant, rich, profound, breathtaking, nestled, in the heart of, renowned, transformative (as decoration).
- **AI vocabulary:** delve, tapestry, landscape (abstract), underscore, foster, garner, intricate, pivotal, crucial, showcase, robust, elevate, unlock, embark, navigate (figurative).
- **`-ing` pseudo-depth:** trailing "…, highlighting/ensuring/reflecting/fostering X." End the sentence instead.
- **Forced rule of three:** an occasional triplet is fine; do not make every list a trio for rhythm.
- **Negative parallelism:** "It's not just X, it's Y," "not a tool but a mirror." Say what it is.
- **Copula avoidance:** "serves as / functions as / stands as." Use is / are / has.
- **Aphorism formulas:** "X is the language of Y," "X becomes a trap." Make the concrete claim.
- **Persuasive-authority tropes:** "the real question is," "at its core," "what really matters," "fundamentally."
- **Signposting:** "let's dive in," "here's what you need to know," "let's break this down." Just say it.
- **Manufactured drama:** a run of clipped fragments to sound dramatic. One short sentence for emphasis is fine; a stack is not.
- **Fake-candor openers:** standalone "Honestly?", "Look,", "Here's the thing."
- **Filler and hedging:** "in order to" to "to"; "due to the fact that" to "because"; drop "it's important to note that"; cut "could potentially possibly."
- **Generic upbeat conclusions:** "Exciting times lie ahead." End on something concrete.
- **Chatbot artifacts:** "I hope this helps," "Would you like me to…," "Great question!"

### Keep these (human signals)

- Vary sentence length: short, then longer.
- Specific, concrete detail over rounded generalities.
- Honest uncertainty and stated limits (already core to the brand).
- First person and real opinion where the piece calls for it.
- The brand formula holds: *You think X. Actually, Y. That is why Z.*
