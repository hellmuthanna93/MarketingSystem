## Observed traits (with evidence)

### 1. Opens on the reader's lived experience, not background

> "You think burnout means you cannot get out of bed. For many creative and ambitious people, it looks different: you still deliver, still smile in meetings, still answer emails at midnight. The collapse is quieter."

The signature **You think X… actually Y** move appears in the wild. No "in today's world", no credentials up front.

### 2. Names the pattern with precise, concrete detail

> "Chronic tension even on vacation / Irritability toward people you love, followed by shame / Numbness or cynicism where passion used to live / Fear that slowing down will expose you as a fraud"

Specifics make the reader think *"that's me."* Calibrate new lists this concretely.

### 3. The core thesis, stated plainly

> "Understanding is not the same as release." / "Insight alone rarely changes the body's habit of bracing for the next demand."

This is the positioning in her own words. Reuse the *idea*, vary the phrasing.

### 4. Reframes, doesn't pathologize

> "That is not a motivation problem. It is often the nervous system refusing to keep performing a life that costs too much on the inside."

Warm, grounded reframe; no blame, no clinical coldness.

### 5. Credentials appear as lived story, late and humble

> "As someone who moved from surgical medicine through my own burnout into psychology, I care about the whole picture…"

Authority is shown through experience, not listed as a flex — and it comes *after* the recognition, not before.

### 6. Closes with possibility + soft, optional invitation

> "Burnout loosens when you stop living as a project and start living as a person." / "If this resonates, you do not have to face it alone."

Ends on an image and a no-pressure door, not a hard CTA.

## Vocabulary actually used

release, stuck, pattern, nervous system, regulation, self-worth, present, resonate, the whole picture, reclaim, inhabitable, a life that costs too much on the inside.

## Rhythm (annotated)

Anna mixes lengths deliberately:

- Blunt: *"Understanding is not the same as release."* (6 words)
- Flowing: *"Psychological counseling creates space to go deeper than analysis: to process what your mind catalogued but your body still holds, to rebuild self-worth that does not depend on output…"* (long, breathing)

New copy should do the same — never all-medium-length sentences (a key AI tell; see `humanizer-patterns.md`).

## Reusable voice prompt

> Write as Anna Hellmuth. Open on the reader's real, specific experience using "you". Name the pattern precisely (concrete details they'll recognize). State one grounded reframe — change is possible, insight alone isn't enough. Speak as an equal who has been there; mention credentials only as lived story, late, never as a flex. Mix sentence lengths: at least one blunt short line and one flowing long one. End on an image or a soft, optional invitation — never pressure. No slogans, no hype, no "research shows", no emoji.

## So what

This is the calibration target. When a draft drifts toward generic coach-speak, compare it against these real samples and the reusable prompt above. Enforced operationally by the `practice-voice` skill.
