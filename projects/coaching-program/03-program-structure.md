## Phase framework: working draft

Anna's concept has a clear psychological sequence:

1. Understand the life and patterns that brought the client here.
2. Discover what fits and choose a new direction.
3. Grow into the person who can live that direction.
4. Meet real life, act, adapt, and continue growing.

The phases should remain connected rather than rigid. A client may meet an old
pattern while taking action, or discover something new about their values while
testing a decision.

### Phase 1: Understand the old chapter

**Purpose:** Assess the present situation, understand how the client built this
life, and see why it made sense at the time.

Possible work:

- Assess the client's current life, dissatisfaction, transition, and sources of
  stability.
- Trace the important choices, roles, expectations, and adaptations that shaped
  the current life.
- Identify recurring emotional, relational, and decision-making patterns.
- Understand what those patterns protected or made possible.
- See both the benefits and the costs of the old way of living.
- Connect past causes with present outcomes without blame.
- Begin distinguishing an old anxiety pattern from a genuine signal that
  something no longer fits.
- Make space for grief, self-compassion, and a calm goodbye to the old chapter.

Desired end state:

> I understand why I made these choices and why I arrived here. Given who I was,
> what I knew, and what I was trying to protect, my path makes sense. I can stop
> blaming myself and say goodbye to this chapter with compassion.

This is stronger and less absolute than saying "it could not have gone any
other way." It preserves self-compassion without removing the client's agency.

### Phase 2: Choose the new direction

**Purpose:** Know the self more deeply, redefine success, and build a direction
that fits the client's values, needs, strengths, and real life.

Possible work:

- Develop a new level of self-acceptance, care, and honest self-knowledge.
- Clarify values, needs, strengths, interests, energy, and preferred ways of
  working and living.
- Notice which desires are genuinely personal and which come from expectation,
  status, fear, or comparison.
- Redefine success in the client's own terms.
- Explore possible futures and the life conditions each one would require.
- Use small experiments, conversations, or research to test assumptions.
- Choose a direction, or define a deliberate exploration path when certainty
  would be premature.
- Align the vision and goals with the client's values and personality.

Desired end state:

> I know what matters to me, what kind of life fits me, and the direction I want
> to explore or choose. This vision belongs to me.

#### Assessments

Personality and strengths assessments can support reflection, but they should
not tell the client who they are or make the decision for them.

- **CliftonStrengths:** A strong optional tool for exploring recurring talents
  and how the client naturally contributes. Gallup publishes technical
  documentation about its design, reliability, and validity. It may be
  recommended when useful, but it is not required or included in the program
  price.
- **16Personalities:** Useful as an accessible conversation starter, especially
  when the client recognizes themselves in the language. It is the NERIS model,
  not the official MBTI, and combines type labels with trait-based scales.
  Treat the result as a hypothesis to explore, not a fixed identity.
- **Other paid assessments:** Optional recommendations only. The client chooses
  whether to purchase them separately.
- **Values work:** Essential. A test cannot replace a careful exploration of
  what the client wants their life to stand for.

Sources:

- [CliftonStrengths technical report](https://support.gallup.com/hc/en-us/articles/43170394336787-CliftonStrengths-Technical-Report)
- [16Personalities framework](https://www.16personalities.com/articles/our-theory)

### Phase 3: Grow into the future self

**Purpose:** Develop the inner patterns, states, thoughts, and behaviors that
can support the new direction.

Possible work:

- Define how the future self relates to uncertainty, responsibility, success,
  visibility, rest, and other people's opinions.
- Identify the beliefs and automatic responses that belong to the old chapter.
- Practice thoughts, emotional responses, decisions, boundaries, and behaviors
  that fit the new direction.
- Build the client's capacity to tolerate expansion without retreating into the
  familiar.
- Learn how to recognize an old pattern when it returns.
- Create a specific response for moments when fear, self-doubt, or
  over-responsibility reappears.
- Use guided hypno-meditations to experience the future self, rehearse new inner
  states, and make the new way of being feel more familiar.
- Practice the future self in small, low-risk situations before taking the
  larger first steps in phase four.

Desired end state:

> I know how I want to meet my life now. I recognize the old pattern when it
> appears, and I know how to choose differently.

The image of "slipping into a new costume" is useful as an experiential
metaphor. For public language, **future self** or **new way of being** may be
safer than **new personality**. The goal is not to perform a different identity
or reject the old self. It is to make new responses feel increasingly natural
and genuinely one's own.

The hypno-meditations happen live during the 1:1 sessions. They are part of
coaching and personal development and are not provided as recordings. They
should not be presented as treatment for anxiety, depression, trauma, or
another clinical condition.

### Phase 4: Become stable in the new chapter and take first steps

**Purpose:** Take the first meaningful steps, become stable in the new patterns,
learn from reality, and remain adaptable.

Possible work:

- Take the first significant steps toward the chosen direction.
- Test ideas and decisions at an appropriate level of risk.
- Meet real reactions from colleagues, family, partners, clients, or the
  market.
- Notice when reality calls for adaptation and when an old fear is asking the
  client to retreat.
- Recover from setbacks without returning automatically to the old identity.
- Adjust the direction without treating adaptation as failure.
- Strengthen the new patterns through repeated action.
- Create a realistic transition plan without an artificial time limit.
- Define what will support the change after the core program.

Desired end state:

> I am taking the first real steps into my new chapter. I can stay stable when
> old patterns return, learn from what happens, and adapt without abandoning my
> direction or myself.

The complimentary follow-up session takes place one month after this phase so
the client returns with real experience.

## Important scope boundary

Looking into the past can be part of coaching when it helps the client
understand present patterns and make future decisions. The program should remain
framed as coaching support, pattern work, and future-oriented change. It should
not promise trauma processing, diagnosis, treatment of anxiety or depression,
or psychotherapy. If deeper clinical needs emerge, pause and consider
psychological counseling or an appropriate referral.

## Between-session support

The program should include enough contact to support action without creating an
unlimited-access service that is difficult to sustain.

Confirmed:

- The client may send one written message per week between sessions.
- Anna replies within two business days.
- One focused assignment at a time.
- Assignments can include reflection, a conversation, research, a small
  visibility action, or a decision experiment.
- No crisis or emergency support.
- No unlimited messaging.

This gives the client continuity and gives Anna control over workload.

## Useful program materials

Keep the materials small and personal. The value is the work with Anna, not a
large content library.

Recommended:

- Pre-program reflection questionnaire.
- Current-life and transition map.
- Stability and fear map.
- Old-pattern decision map.
- Personal definition of success.
- Decision criteria or "decision compass."
- Experiment tracker.
- Transition strategy.
- Final next-chapter transition plan.

## Why the confirmed duration is sellable

The offer is finite, specific, and substantial:

- Four months feels serious enough for meaningful change without sounding
  indefinite.
- The client does not need to promise a completed career change, move, or
  business launch within the program.
- The final result is concrete: a chosen direction, changed decision patterns,
  first actions, and a transition plan that can unfold at the client's pace.
- The complimentary follow-up extends support beyond the formal ending and
  gives the client a place to review what happened once they continued on their
  own.

The program should be marketed through the client's movement, not through the
number of calls or worksheets.

## Research notes

Research does not identify one scientifically optimal number of coaching
sessions. A 2023 workplace-coaching meta-analysis found positive overall effects
but did not find that session count or total coaching hours reliably predicted
outcomes. This means the structure should follow the client's problem and the
mechanism of change rather than a marketing claim about a magic duration.

A 2024 scoping review found coaching interventions ranging from two to 16
sessions, with a median of 10. Sessions were commonly weekly or fortnightly and
often ran over 6 to 12 weeks. A separate review of sustained health and wellness
coaching outcomes found a mean of 8.1 sessions across interventions, but those
programs varied widely in duration and purpose.

Habit research is not direct evidence for life-transition coaching, but it is a
useful caution against very short transformation promises. A 2024 systematic
review found wide individual variation and suggested that even relatively
simple health habits often need two to five months to become more automatic.
Identity, career, and life-direction changes are more complex than those habits.

Sources:

- [Workplace coaching meta-analysis, Frontiers in Psychology](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1204166/full)
- [Coaching scoping review, Australian Occupational Therapy Journal](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11609348/)
- [Sustained gains from health and wellness coaching](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10979731/)
- [Habit formation systematic review and meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11641623/)
